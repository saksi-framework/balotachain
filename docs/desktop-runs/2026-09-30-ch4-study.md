## 2. Findings to carry into the paper

### (a) Throughput falls with scale, which contradicts CLAIMS.md's "≈ 920–1000 TPS"

The median falls from about 900 TPS at 1K (893.9 SP, 924.6 MP) through 885.8 (SP-10K) and
789.3 (MP-10K) to 697.3 (SP-50K) and **570.3 (MP-50K)**, while p99 rises from 208–228 ms to
524 ms and p50 from 144 to 219 ms. `docs/CLAIMS.md` line 45 states "Sustained committed
throughput on this desktop ≈ 920–1000 TPS", citing SP-1K 995 and MP-1K 952 from earlier
builds; on `4a38a54` only MP-1K reaches the band, and every tier from MP-10K up is well below
it. CLAIMS.md was not edited; the claim needs revising against these rows.

The fall tracks ledger size. Each attempt starts on an empty ledger, and its two warm-ups run
first. From 10K up the warm-ups beat the measured median in 6 of 7 campaigns: SP-10K 966 / 950
against 886; SP-50K 765 / 782 against 697 (attempt 1: 716 / 742 against 659); MP-50K 633 / 584
against 570; MP-10K attempt 1 855 / 876 against 705; the exception is MP-10K's standing
attempt (825 / 745 against 789). Within the measured repetitions the decline is clear in one
campaign only, MP-10K attempt 1 (808 and 827 TPS, then 627–777, p50 rising 149 to 183 ms);
the standing MP-10K attempt does not repeat it. At 1K the warm-ups are the slow ones (cold
start). So: **across tiers the fall is large and consistent; the within-campaign decline as the
ledger grows is suggested, not established.**

For comparison, the parallel-auditor baseline on saksi `ef663d1`
([`2026-09-12-rows2-4-parallel.md`](2026-09-12-rows2-4-parallel.md) §7) measured the same six
tiers at 1004.09, 1011.79, 968.99, 954.69, 933.32 and 793.02 TPS. `4a38a54` is lower by 11,
9, 9, 17, 25 and 28 % respectively, the gap widening with scale. This study does not identify
the cause (the builds differ, including saksi #50's random DKG dealers); it should be
investigated before the two sets are compared in the paper.

### (b) The wizard's contention rule flags every run from 10K up, on the run's own load

The rule marks a repetition contended if host CPU exceeds 25 % **or** guest `load1` exceeds
0.25 x 16 = 4.0. The guest's 1-minute load average includes the run's own generate and verify
(16 auditor threads; 1.8 s of in-process verification per SP-10K repetition, 26 s per MP-50K
one), sampled seconds later. So **all 30 measured repetitions of the four standing tiers from
10K up are flagged, every one on guest load**, with load1 4.9 to 13.9 rising with tier size,
while host CPU flagged only one of them (SP-50K m5's end sample, 25.5 %). Across all 11
campaigns, 12 of the 190 host CPU samples on measured repetitions exceed 25 % (6 %), and 7 of the
12 are at 1K, where a busy desktop is the plausible source. The highest host
sample on any standing 10K+ campaign is 25.5 %; the others peak at 24.6, 19.8 and 21.2 %.

This is an **instrument finding**: the guest half of the rule cannot tell self-load from
outside load, so it drove reruns at SP-10K, SP-50K and MP-50K that no host contention
justified. The rule, or the sample timing, needs to exclude the run's own work before
"contended" can be cited as evidence of interference.

### (c) Occasional in-network stalls with a quiet host

Four measured repetitions show multi-second stalls (a `ballots.progress` checkpoint gap far
above the tier's usual 1–3 s) and a p99 of 0.7 to 2.9 s, with host CPU samples of 2.4 to
18.2 %:

load; this study does not pin it down (no orderer or peer logs were kept for these windows).
