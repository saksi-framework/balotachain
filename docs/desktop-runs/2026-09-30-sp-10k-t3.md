## 3. Recovery

- **Nothing was lost.** Every one of the 10,000 ballots is on the chain exactly once:
  verify-only reconciled the chain's count against the run's committed set with 0 missing, and
  the chain walk passed over 204 linked blocks.
- **The tally is exact.** E = 0 and `pass` true on all 4 contests, on both the `source=local`
  and `source=ledger` rows; `ledger_matches_local` true on all 8 rows; ledger dump 10,000
  ballots (`batched`).
- **The peer came back in about 21.3 s**: the 20 s planned outage (20,255 ms measured) plus
  1,054 ms until the window's connection was ready.
- **The chain kept committing through the stop.** At `fault.start` the chain held 3,893;
  the driver saw 4,000 commit, and 45 more landed with errors returned to the driver, so 4,045
  were on the chain when the resume began. The resume submitted only the 5,955 the chain did
  not hold; none were replays.
- **Resume timing is the driver's, not the system's.** The 51 s between `fault.end` and the
  resume reflects `drive.py`'s polling, and the 20–30 s gaps between later steps likewise; the
  resumed segment itself took 8.2 s.

## 4. Caveats

- **Closed-loop drop count.** With `send_rate` 0, the workers drain the rest of the window as
  fast as refusals come back, so every ballot after the fault point drops within about a second
  of the stop (runbook §10.6). The 6,000 is "the rest of the window", not "ballots lost over a
  20 s outage". Measuring outage-window loss needs a fixed send rate; this run did not do that.
- One fault, one duration (20 s), one peer, at one point (40 %). The orderer and the other
  peer (`peer0.org2`) stayed up; the driver's peer check at 02:43 local found all three
  containers Up afterwards.
- `perf.csv`'s 802.241 TPS and p99 249.7 ms describe the first segment only (4,000 ballots to
  the fault); `sustained` is false. Not for RQ3.
- The study log's account of this run matches the files in every figure checked.
