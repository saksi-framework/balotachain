## 4. Rate sweep (T2, saturation)

**`campaign-20260930-205246-12`** (`done`, 20:52:46Z to 21:40:01Z): 0 warm-ups + 0 measured, `sweep` 1.6, `window_s` 120, `burst` 144,900; config `send_rate` 250. It ran on the same network after the measured campaign.

**Why a second campaign.** A campaign's `config.send_rate` is both the sweep's first offered rate and the rate cap of every warm-up and measured repetition: `Repeat` passes one `Config` to both. Following runbook §10.4 literally, with Send rate set on the tier's one campaign, would have run the 1 + 3 repetitions open-loop at the sweep's start rate, not closed-loop like rows 2–4. The two campaigns keep the order `Repeat` itself uses: measured, then sweep, then burst.

**Values chosen, and why:**
- **Send rate 250 /s, factor 1.6.** The steps offer 250, 400, 640, 1,024, 1,638, … /s. Rows 2–4 committed 570–925 TPS, falling with scale, so SP-483K was expected to saturate near 500–650. The first two steps sit below that and should climb. Step 3 is at the knee. Step 4 (1,024 /s) is over 900 and over the network's throughput.
- **Window 120 s**, the driver default. At 1,024 /s a step offers about 123K ballots, under the 483K each step's election holds. Each step also pays a full 483K generate, so a longer window buys little.

**`plateau_tps` = 610.928** (`sweep/summary.csv`): step 4, offered 1,024 /s. Step 5 at 1,638 /s committed less (595.7), so the sweep stopped there. No step dropped a ballot. The plateau was found above the offered rate the brief asked for: the top step offered 1,638 /s, the plateau step 1,024 /s.

Past the knee, latency is queueing:
- p50 goes from 261 ms at 640 /s to 2.0 s at 1,024 /s and 9.7 s at 1,638 /s;
- p99 goes from 1.3 s to 3.5 s and 11.9 s;
- the concurrency each step sizes from the previous p99 (Little's law) grows to 5,777 in flight.

The plateau (611) is 20 % above the closed-loop measured median (509) and 15 % above the fastest measured repetition (533). The steps are 120 s windows with up to 5,777 in flight, against 128 in flight over 15–18 minutes, and they come after the measured repetitions on an even larger ledger. So the closed-loop figure understates the network's saturation throughput at this scale by about 15–20 %.

**Sweep-step correctness.** Every step is a time-bounded window (`run.end` `bounded: true`, `failed: false`, `sustained: false`), so only part of each step's 483K ballots reaches the ledger. The `source=local` rows are E = 0 and pass. The `source=ledger` rows read `pass false` and `ledger_matches_local false`, which is expected for a bounded window. It is not a failure, and `runs_failed` is 0.

## 5. Peak burst (T8)

**Sizing.** Zamboanga City has 483,058 voters. Over a 10-hour voting day that averages 48,300 per hour. The worst-case hour is taken as three times that, 30 % of the electorate in one hour: **144,900 ballots**, sent unthrottled as one election (`send_rate` 0, concurrency 128). Delivering a whole hour's worst-case load at once is harsher than the real arrival process.

## 7. Caveats

- Three measured repetitions, with a clear downward drift across them, so the median depends on how far the ledger has grown.
- One sweep. Each step's window is 120 s, so the plateau is a short-window saturation figure.
- The burst size rests on an assumed peak-hour factor of 3.
- Host CPU was never above 10 % on a measured sample, but the host is sampled at two points per repetition only.
- One build, one machine, one single-orderer network.
