## 3. Concurrency probe

The procedure's default concurrency (8) does not measure this network. At c = 8 every
ballot waits out the orderer's 2 s `BatchTimeout`, because 8 in flight never fills a
block (`MaxMessageCount` 10): `committed_tps` 3.92 against a `driver_ceiling_tps` of
3.93, p50 2,037 ms with a standard deviation of 4–15 ms. The instrument says so itself
— `scaling_limit: inconclusive` on every rep. Those five reps are in
`superseded-c8/` and in §6; they are **not** row-2 figures.

### Probe 1 — at 1,000 voters (superseded: window too short)

| c | committed | dropped | submit window | committed_tps | driver_ceiling_tps | p50 ms | p95 ms | p99 ms | peer CPU % | scaling_limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 1000 | 0 | 254,798 ms | 3.925 | 3.926 | 2037.6 | 2044.9 | 2048.0 | 5.66 | inconclusive |
| 32 | 1000 | 0 | 3,164 ms | 316.031 | 339.720 | 94.2 | 139.0 | 182.5 | *(empty)* | inconclusive |
| 64 | 1000 | 0 | 4,627 ms | 216.095 | 391.452 | 163.5 | 217.7 | 244.9 | *(empty)* | false |

Discarded as a basis for the decision: at 1,000 voters the submission window is only
3–5 s, so a single rep is dominated by ramp-up and by whichever block boundary it
straddles, and the container sampler never ticks — `peak_cpu_pct_peer` and
`peak_cpu_pct_orderer` come back **empty**, which the honest-CSV rule writes rather
than a zero. The 316 → 216 fall is inside that noise.

### Probe 2 — at 10,000 voters (decisive)

One rep each, no warm-up, no tier reset between steps; windows of 21–75 s give the
5 s sampler 4–15 ticks.

| c | committed | dropped | submit window | committed_tps | driver_ceiling_tps | 0.8 × ceiling | p50 ms | p95 ms | p99 ms | peer CPU % | orderer CPU % | scaling_limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | 10000 | 0 | 75,336 ms | 132.738 | 436.975 | 349.58 | 54.9 | 95.2 | 1702.7 | 183.36 | 47.45 | false |
| 32 | 10000 | 0 | 24,996 ms | 400.059 | 445.246 | 356.20 | 71.9 | 117.1 | 179.4 | 210.79 | 56.78 | inconclusive |
| 48 | 10000 | 0 | 21,245 ms | 470.684 | 500.442 | 400.35 | 95.9 | 152.3 | 220.8 | 237.25 | 60.52 | inconclusive |
| 64 | 10000 | 0 | 40,578 ms | 246.437 | 503.199 | 402.56 | 127.2 | 233.2 | 5381.4 | 235.13 | 59.64 | false |
| 96 | 10000 | 0 | 21,079 ms | 474.389 | 504.490 | 403.59 | 190.3 | 279.0 | 441.7 | 236.56 | 60.25 | inconclusive |

No step drops a ballot; `failed=false` and `fail_reason` empty in all five.

**No c satisfies the winner rule in full.** The two steps that clear
`committed_tps < 0.8 × driver_ceiling_tps` (c = 24 and c = 64) clear it only because a
multi-second stall dragged their throughput down — see their p99s of 1.7 s and 5.4 s
against a p95 of 95 ms and 233 ms. The three clean steps (32, 48, 96) all sit above
0.8 × ceiling, i.e. **the closed-loop driver could not be ruled out as the limit at any
concurrency tried**. Per the fallback, the winner is the c with the highest
`committed_tps`: **c = 96** (474.389). c = 48 is statistically indistinguishable
(470.684, 0.8 % lower) at half the concurrency and half the p50, so nothing below
turns on the choice between them.

Peer CPU plateaus at ~236 % of one core (of 1,600 % available) from c = 48 upward, and
orderer CPU at ~60 %, so the ceiling being hit is not this machine's CPU.

## 7. Recomputed hours for rows 4–9

`submissions = voters × positions`; `hours = submissions / committed_tps / 3600`, at
the measured medians: **SP 490.468 TPS**, **MP 494.267 TPS** (both at c = 96).

The submission window is not the run. At SP-1K a rep's submission window is 2.03 s of a
**59 s** rep; at MP-1K, 6.06 s of a **157 s** rep — 3.4 % and 3.9 %. The remainder is
per-ballot work outside the timed region (generation, proof verification, the ledger
dump and its audit, artifact writing) and it scales with ballots: **59 ms/ballot at
SP-1K, 52 ms/ballot at MP-1K**, against ~2 ms/ballot of submission. Both columns are
given; the second is the one that decides what fits in a day.

Row 5's sweep is `--sweep k` with `--window` (default 120 s) per step: its cost is
`steps × window`, not a function of TPS, so no hours figure is derived for it here.

**Row 8 is not estimable from these artifacts.** Offline runs open no submission
window, so the formula does not apply, and no offline tier was run at c = 96. The one
offline datapoint this session produced is the ladder's 1,000-voter × 3-position tier,
which processed 3,000 ballots in 763 s — **3.93 ballots/s at concurrency 8**, the same
rate as the on-chain c = 8 runs, at a load average of 0.24. Whether offline scales with
concurrency the way the on-chain path did (×125 from c = 8 to c = 96) is unknown and
must not be assumed. One offline MP tier at c = 96 would settle it, cheaply.

**What drops.** Against the full-run column, rows 4 and 5 fit (5.7 + 15.3 + 31.7 ≈
53 h), and everything below does not: row 6 alone is 65.6 h, row 7 is 178 h and row 9 is
152 h — 448 h ≈ 18.7 days for rows 4–9 together. Dropping from the bottom: **row 9 goes
first, then row 7, then row 6.** Rows 4 and 5 are the affordable ones.

The constraint is **not the chain**. Submission is under 4 % of a run; the other 96 % is
the per-ballot generate / verify / ledger-audit path, on 16 vCPU with 15 GB of RAM. The
cheap ways to buy the big tiers back are therefore (a) an offline row-8-style path where
the chain is skipped, (b) checkpointing (already supported for rows 7 and 9), or
(c) a machine with the full 32 GB and no WSL split. Reshaping the table is a decision
for the user; nothing here is silently reshaped.

## 8. Caveats and what the instrument refused

- **The citable TPS comes from 10,000 voters and above.** Row-2's own submission
  windows are only 2–6 s long (`submit_window_ms` 2,027 for SP-1K, 6,062 for MP-1K), so
  the row-2 figures are quotable as per-rep results across 10 reps but the throughput
  they measure is corroborated by the 21–75 s windows of the 10K probe, where the
  sampler also produced non-empty CPU peaks.
- **Both tiers report `scaling_limit: inconclusive`**, because `committed_tps` is within
  20 % of `driver_ceiling_tps` (96.7 % and 98.0 % of it). 490 / 494 TPS are lower bounds
  on the system, not measured system limits.
- **The ladder gate refused nothing** — it passed on this build and unlocked tiers above
  1,000 voters. The disk guard did not fire (12 MB and 36 MB projected against 946 GB
  free). The offline voter ceiling was never approached.
- **`ledger_bytes_delta` is empty in every on-chain run of this session** (SP-1K, MP-1K
  and all seven probe runs). Per `perf-schema.md` an empty cell means the column had no
  producer for this run; it is not a measured zero. The ledger-size figure that rows 4–9
  are sized against therefore has no artifact here.
- **`peak_cpu_pct_peer` / `peak_cpu_pct_orderer` are empty for SP-1K and MP-1K** — the
  sampler ticks every 5 s and these windows are 2–6 s. The 10K probe rows carry the CPU
  peaks instead (peer ~236 %, orderer ~60 % of one core).
- **`negative-tests.csv` was produced by no run in this session.** The `--repeat` driver
  runs generate → check → submit → ceremony → verify; the Scenarios stage that writes
  that file (and `scenarios/`) is not on that path, so the artifact simply does not
  exist for these runs. It is listed in the brief's copy set and is absent for that
  reason, not because it was dropped.
- The only instrument failure this session was the Fabric bring-up on a machine holding
  a two-week-old ledger, verbatim:

  ```
  Error: proposal failed (err: bad proposal response 500: cannot create ledger from
  genesis block: ledger [saksi] already exists with state [ACTIVE])
  After 5 attempts, peer0.org1 has failed to join channel 'saksi'
    ✗ network bring-up failed. Try: ./tools/up.sh down, then retry.
  ```

  Resolved by the remedy the message names (`./tools/up.sh down`, then retry), which
  removed only the three `compose_*` Fabric volumes, the `fabric_test` network and the
  two generated `dev-peer0.*-saksi-bulletin` images. Unrelated Docker resources on this
  box were verified untouched.
- **`docs/manuscript-amendments.md` was deliberately NOT edited.** WSL allotted 15 of
  the machine's 32 GB while Table 3.12 specifies 32, so the numbers land here with that
  caveat and the user decides what enters the manuscript.
