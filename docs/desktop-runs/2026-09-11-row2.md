## 3. Concurrency probe

The procedure's default concurrency (8) does not measure this network. At c = 8 every
ballot waits out the orderer's 2 s `BatchTimeout`, because 8 in flight never fills a
block (`MaxMessageCount` 10): `committed_tps` 3.92 against a `driver_ceiling_tps` of
3.93, p50 2,037 ms with a standard deviation of 4–15 ms. The instrument says so itself
— `scaling_limit: inconclusive` on every rep. Those five reps are in
`superseded-c8/` and in §6; they are **not** row-2 figures.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 1000 | 0 | 254,798 ms | 3.925 | 3.926 | 2037.6 | 2044.9 | 2048.0 | 5.66 | inconclusive |
| 32 | 1000 | 0 | 3,164 ms | 316.031 | 339.720 | 94.2 | 139.0 | 182.5 | *(empty)* | inconclusive |

`peak_cpu_pct_orderer` come back **empty**, which the honest-CSV rule writes rather
than a zero. The 316 → 216 fall is inside that noise.

**No c satisfies the winner rule in full.** The two steps that clear

0.8 × ceiling, i.e. **the closed-loop driver could not be ruled out as the limit at any
concurrency tried**. Per the fallback, the winner is the c with the highest
`committed_tps`: **c = 96** (474.389). c = 48 is statistically indistinguishable
(470.684, 0.8 % lower) at half the concurrency and half the p50, so nothing below
turns on the choice between them.

Peer CPU plateaus at ~236 % of one core (of 1,600 % available) from c = 48 upward, and
orderer CPU at ~60 %, so the ceiling being hit is not this machine's CPU.

## 7. Recomputed hours for rows 4–9

### The cost model, derived from the artifacts

The procedure's `hours = submissions / committed_tps / 3600` covers the submission
window only, and the submission window is a small part of a run: 2.03 s of a 58 s
SP-1K rep, 6.06 s of a 154 s MP-1K rep. The rest is **not** per-ballot overhead. Read
from the journals and `receipts.csv`, a run costs three separable things.

**(a) A fixed on-chain lifecycle, per run, independent of the tier.** Around the ballot
window the console runs `CreateElection`, `PublishDKGTranscript`, `CloseElection`, one
`SubmitPartialDecryption` per (trustee × position × candidate), and `PublishTally`.
Each is a single transaction, alone in its own block, and each waits out the orderer's
2 s `BatchTimeout`. From `sp-1k-20260910-200743-3/receipts.csv`, the 20 partials land at
`20:07:52, :54, :56, … :08:30` — **exactly 2 s apart** — and
`docker logs orderer.example.com | grep "Created block"` shows the same cadence
(`21:00:14.751, :16.773, :18.795, …`, 2.02 s apart). The transaction count is
`4 + trustees × positions × candidates`:

| Tier | partials | lifecycle tx | predicted 2 s × tx | measured (`CreateElection` → next stage) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SP-1K (5 trustees, 1 position, 4 candidates) | 20 | 24 | 48 s | **48 s** (`20:07:43` → `20:08:34`, of which `20:07:50` → `20:08:32` is the post-window part) |
| MP-1K (5 × 3 × 4) | 60 | 64 | 128 s | **128 s** (`20:25:22` → `20:27:36` + 2 s) |
| SP-10K probe (5 × 1 × 4) | 20 | 24 | 48 s | **~45 s** (`CloseElection 20:01:51` → `PublishTally 20:02:34`) |

The 10K probe pays the same lifecycle as SP-1K with **ten times the ballots**, which is
the proof that it is fixed per run, not per ballot. It dominates a 1,000-voter rep and
is negligible at 483,000.

Blocks are cut at the configured `MaxMessageCount` during the ballot window, not by the
timeout: distinct `block_number` values in `receipts.csv` are 124 for SP-1K (100 ballot
blocks = 1000/10, plus 24 lifecycle blocks), 364 for MP-1K (300 + 64) and 1,024 for the
10K probe (1,000 + 24). Receipt fetching is therefore ~100 `qscc` calls per 1,000
ballots and is not a cost worth modelling.

**(b) Verification, linear in ballots, ~4.9 ms/ballot.** `stage.verify` measures
5,026 ms at 1,000 ballots, 14,366 ms at 3,000 and 48,580 ms at 10,000 — 5.03, 4.79 and
4.86 ms/ballot. It is the ledger dump (one `GetBallot` per nullifier) plus two
audit-stream passes, one over the console's own artifacts and one over the ledger copy;
`timings.json` `verify_ballots` is 1.63 / 1.44 / 1.56 ms/ballot for a single pass.

**(c) Submission, ~2.0 ms/ballot** at c = 96 (the reciprocal of the measured 490.5 and
494.3 TPS), plus **generation, ~0.18 ms/ballot** (`gen-timings.json` `wall_ms`: 186 ms
for SP-1K's 1,000 ballots, 497 ms for MP-1K's 3,000, 1,844 ms for the probe's 10,000,
4,894 ms for 30,000 offline).

So, per run:

```
run_seconds ≈ ballots × (2.0 + 4.9 + 0.2) ms  +  2 s × (4 + trustees × positions × candidates)
            ≈ ballots × 7.1 ms               +  48 s (SP) / 128 s (MP)
```

Checked against the measured rep wall clocks: SP-1K predicts 55 s against 58 s measured,
MP-1K 149 s against 154 s, the SP-10K probe 119 s against 126 s — 3–6 % low, the
remainder being the ceremony and per-run bookkeeping.

## 8. Caveats and what the instrument refused

- **The citable TPS comes from 10,000 voters and above.** Row-2's own submission
  windows are only 2–6 s long (`submit_window_ms` 2,027 for SP-1K, 6,062 for MP-1K), so
  the row-2 figures are quotable as per-rep results across 10 reps but the throughput
  they measure is corroborated by the 21–75 s windows of the 10K probe, where the
  sampler also produced non-empty CPU peaks.
- **Both tiers report `scaling_limit: inconclusive`**, because `committed_tps` is within
  20 % of `driver_ceiling_tps` (96.7 % and 98.0 % of it). 490 / 494 TPS are lower bounds
  on the system, not measured system limits.
- **The ladder gate refused nothing** — it passed on this build and unlocked tiers above
  1,000 voters. The disk guard did not fire (12 MB and 36 MB projected against 946 GB
  free). The offline voter ceiling was never approached.
- **The ladder exercised the on-chain path, not an offline one — a stronger result than
  the gate promises.** Its run configs say `"mode": "offline"`, but
  `validation-ladder-20260910-190037-4/receipts.csv` holds 3,000 `SubmitBallot` rows plus
  `CreateElection`, `PublishDKGTranscript`, `CloseElection`, 36 `SubmitPartialDecryption`
  and `PublishTally`, on block numbers 169 → 583 of the live chain, with the lifecycle
  rows 2 s apart. Its `stage.ballots.start` records `concurrency: 8`, and its 3,000
  ballots took 763 s — 3.93 ballots/s, the same `BatchTimeout`-bound rate as the
  superseded concurrency-8 SP-1K reps. **So all four ladder tiers (1, 10, 100, 1,000
  voters) passed with E = 0 against real chaincode on a real ledger.** What `offline`
  actually suppressed is the ledger audit: the ladder's `correctness.csv` carries
  `source=local` rows only.
- **No offline throughput datapoint existed until one was made for §7.** Because
  `mode: "offline"` still transacts against a Fabric-wired console, sizing row 8 needed a
  console started without the `--fabric-*` flags; that run is in §7 and its artifacts are
  in `probe/`.
- **`ledger_bytes_delta` is empty in every on-chain run of this session** (SP-1K, MP-1K
  and all seven probe runs). Per `perf-schema.md` an empty cell means the column had no
  producer for this run; it is not a measured zero. The ledger-size figure that rows 4–9
  are sized against therefore has no artifact here — the §7 hours are time, not bytes.
- **The per-run cost splits cleanly, and the chain is the small half.** Per ballot:
  submission ~2.0 ms, verification ~4.9 ms, generation ~0.2 ms. Per run: a fixed
  on-chain lifecycle of 48 s (SP) or 128 s (MP), which is 83 % of an SP-1K rep and 0.2 %
  of an SP-3.5M run. Nothing here is a per-ballot chain cost beyond the 2 ms; the earlier
  reading of the post-window gap as ~55 ms/ballot of overhead was wrong — that gap is the
  fixed lifecycle, and it does not grow with the tier (the 10K probe pays the same 45 s
  as SP-1K with ten times the ballots).
